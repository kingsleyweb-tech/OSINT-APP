The basics

What is open-source intelligence?

OSINT means collecting and studying information that is already public — web pages, news, public social media profiles, maps and official websites — to answer a question. “Open source” means anyone can find the information, without secret methods, hacking or access to private systems.

01

Public and private information

Public information includes web pages, news articles, public social profiles, map listings and organisations’ own websites. Private information — private messages, private accounts, passwords, bank or phone company records — is outside OSINT and outside this platform.

02

Check before you conclude

Many people share a name or a username, and search results can be old. Good research compares several independent sources and opens the original page before deciding that a result belongs to the subject.

Our rule

The platform only collects public information. It is not a consumer reporting service, does not do surveillance or facial recognition, and never accesses private records.

What it investigates.

People, usernames and organisations — with search pages for social media, news, media, places and trends, keyword alerts by email, and tools to review, analyse and report what you find.

    Where results come from

    Public sources, searched live.

    Searches run through SerpApi on Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, YouTube, Google News, Bing News, Google Images, Bing Images, Google Maps and Google Trends. Usernames are also checked directly on platforms such as GitHub, Reddit, Mastodon and Bluesky, organisations are looked up on Wikidata, and an organisation’s own website is read to build its profile.

    • Searches come from our server; the people you search are not contacted or notified.
    • The same search repeated within 12 hours is answered from a saved copy and costs nothing.
    • Search engines change over time, so results can differ from one day to the next.

    Public sources & platforms.

    Search across publicly indexed social, professional, developer, publishing, video, creative, academic, and web sources using the application's configured search infrastructure.

    Honest coverage & limits
    • The application searches and organizes publicly indexed information from supported platforms and websites.
    • Search engines do not index every single profile or web page instantly.
    • Some platforms enforce private profile settings or limit public web indexing.
    • Password-protected, deleted, or unindexed content is strictly inaccessible.
    • Search results fluctuate based on search provider index caches (SerpApi / Google).

    How it works.

    Seven steps from a name, username or organisation to a checked, source-backed case.

    How results are labelled.

    Every result says how well it matches and why. Labels help you judge — they are not proof.

    Exact match

    The account uses exactly the name or username you searched. The platform notes when the platform itself confirmed the account exists.

    Username variation

    The same letters and numbers written differently (for example humblechild99 for humblechild_99). The page says whether anything links it to your subject.

    Possibly related

    A different name or handle, but with real shared evidence — such as the same display name or website. Kept apart until you check it.

    Other person

    A similar name or handle with nothing linking it to your subject — most likely someone else. Never counted as your subject’s.

    Organisation facts

    Each fact shows “Confirmed by N independent sources”, “Single source” or “Sources disagree”. A detail no source gives is shown as “Not found”.

      How the platform works.

      What happens between typing a name, username or organisation and having a checked, source-backed case — step by step.

      01

      Name, username or organisation

      You start a search

      Choose a Name or Username search. You can add a location or organisation to narrow a common name. In Intelligent mode a misspelt name is checked first, and while you type a name the page can suggest matching organisations.

      Example searchesquery

site:linkedin.com/in "Kwame Mensah"
"humblechild_99" OR …
      02

      Many sources at the same time

      Public sources are searched

      The platform plans a small, fixed set of searches and runs them together: Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo and YouTube through SerpApi, direct checks on platforms such as GitHub, Reddit, Mastodon and Bluesky, and for organisations also Google Maps, Google News, Wikidata and the organisation’s own website. A progress bar shows each source, and you can cancel at any time. The same search within 12 hours is answered from a saved copy.

      03

      Only what matches is kept

      Results are cleaned and checked

      Results that do not name your subject are removed, links are cleaned, and post or video links are traced back to the account that owns them. Each account gets a match label with a reason — Exact match, Username variation, Possibly related or Other person.

      Exact matchUsername variationPossibly relatedOther person
      04

      People and organisations separated

      Look-alikes are kept apart

      Results are grouped into possible people — or an organisation — so that different people with the same name are not mixed. When a search is an organisation, its type is worked out from the sources, and an abbreviation like “UPSA” is matched to its full name only when the sources agree.

      Abbreviationsources agree
      UPSA → full name, only when the sources agree
      05

      Everything in tabs, with sources

      You open a case

      Choosing a person or organisation opens a case with its tabs: Overview, Profiles, Activity, Associations, Sources, Web, News, Images, Location, Contact, Metrics and Audit, plus an Organisation tab for organisations. News, images, places and contact details are gathered automatically when you open their tabs. Every finding keeps its source link, and missing details show as “Not found”.

      06

      Evidence levels, audit trail, export

      You review, record and report

      Open the original sources and mark each result Raw, Relevant or Validated. Every search and change is written to the Audit tab with the time. Track the subject on the People page, re-run the case later to see what changed, set a keyword alert to be emailed when something new is published, export a PDF report (or JSON/CSV), or share a view-only link. Your cases are stored in your own account and only you can see them.

      Home/About

      About the OSINT Investigation Platform.

      A web application that finds public information about people, usernames and organisations, and keeps every finding in one organised case — together with the source it came from.

      The problem we solve

      Open-source intelligence (OSINT) research used to mean opening dozens of browser tabs, writing search after search by hand, and sorting out which results really belong to the person or organisation you are looking for.

      The platform searches many public sources at once — search engines, news, maps, public social media pages, Wikidata and organisations’ own websites. It removes results that do not match, keeps people with a similar name apart, and saves everything into a case you can review, re-run and export.

      Principles
      1. 01Public information only — no private accounts or private messages
      2. 02Every finding keeps a link to its source
      3. 03Missing details are shown as “Not found”, never guessed
      4. 04A full record (audit trail) of every search and change in a case

      At a glance.

      What you can search
      People, usernames, organisations, places, news, media and trends — plus keyword alerts by email
      Where results come from
      Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, YouTube, Google News, Bing News, Google Maps, Google Trends (through SerpApi), Wikidata, public platform checks and official websites
      Who it is for
      Investigators, analysts, researchers and journalists
      Where your work is kept
      In your own account (Firebase) — other users cannot see it

      What the platform does.

      01

      Name and username searches

      Group results into possible people, and label each account as an exact match, a username variation, possibly related or another person — with the reason.

      02

      Organisation profiles

      For companies, schools, universities, government agencies, associations and NGOs: type, official name, website, locations, leadership, units, contact details, social accounts and recent news — each fact checked against several sources.

      03

      Search pages

      For social media posts, news, images and videos, places (with a map) and search trends.

      04

      Cases

      With separate tabs for profiles, activity, links to others, sources, web pages, news, images, locations and contact details, plus metrics and an audit trail. Cases can be re-run, tracked, exported as a PDF report and shared with a view-only link.

      05

      Keyword alerts

      Watch news and social platforms for your keywords on a schedule and email you — only you — when new results appear, each alert with its own page of results.

      06

      Analysis

      Of connections between cases and of the content saved in them.

      Responsible use and limits.

      The platform only uses information that is already public. It does not know for certain that two accounts belong to the same person: match labels and confidence levels help you judge, but they are not proof. Always open the original source before you reach a conclusion.

      The platform does not access private accounts, private messages or paid databases, and it does not do facial recognition.

      Not a consumer reporting agency

      The platform is not a Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA) under the FCRA and must not be used for credit, employment, housing or insurance decisions.

      System documentation & guide.

      How every search works, what the results mean, how cases and history are kept, and how the platform protects your account and data.

      Platform Introduction

      The OSINT Investigation Platform is an open-source intelligence workspace for investigators, analysts, journalists and researchers. It searches public sources for a person, username, organisation, place or topic and organises what it finds into cases.

      Searches run live through SerpApi (Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, YouTube, News, Images, Maps and Trends) and free public platform APIs (GitHub, Reddit, Mastodon, Bluesky and others). Nothing is invented: when nothing is found, the platform says so.

      Getting Started

      1. Open the sign-in page and choose Continue with Google (or sign in with email and password).
      2. Start from the Dashboard or New Investigation: choose Name or Username (organisations are found with a Name search), and optionally add a location or organisation.
      3. Watch the progress loader: every engine shows its own status, and you can cancel at any time.
      4. Pick the person or organisation that matches your subject to create a case.
      5. Review the case tabs, save extra findings from the Social, News, Media, Geo and Trends pages, and export a PDF report or share a view-only link when you are done.
      6. Optionally, create a keyword alert on the Alerts page to be emailed when new results about your topic are published.

      What we store, what is sent to search services, and how your work is kept private.

      Effective date · set by the live page7 sections
      Contents
        Contents

          01Scope

          This policy covers the OSINT Investigation Platform. The platform searches information that is already public — search engines, news, maps, public social media pages, Wikidata and organisations’ own websites. We do not buy, build or sell private databases about people.

          02Information we store about you

          To run your workspace we keep only what is needed:

          • Account: your email address, display name and how you sign in (email and password, or Google). Sign-in is handled by Google Firebase — the platform never sees or stores your password.
          • Your work: your investigations (cases) and what they contain — profiles, pages, news, images, places and contact details found, your evidence levels (Raw, Relevant, Validated) and each case’s audit log.
          • Activity in the app: your search history and saved results, the people and organisations you track, your keyword alerts and the results they found, your notifications, and your settings (such as your default search mode). When you share a case, a view-only copy of it is kept until you stop sharing.
          • On your device: your browser keeps your sign-in session, some page state (so results stay when you switch pages) and preferences such as light or dark mode.

          03What is sent to other services

          To find public information, your search terms are sent by our server to:

          • SerpApi, which runs the searches on Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, YouTube, Google News, Bing News, Google Images, Bing Images, Google Maps and Google Trends.
          • Public platform services (such as GitHub, Reddit, Mastodon and Bluesky) for username checks.
          • Wikidata, for organisation names.
          • Brevo, our email provider, which delivers keyword-alert emails. It receives your account email address and the alert results being sent to you — nothing else.
          • Organisations’ own websites, whose public pages our server reads to build an organisation profile.

          These requests come from our server, not your browser. Your name, email address and account are not attached to them, and the people or organisations you search for are not contacted or notified.

          04How long search results are kept

          To save searches, the server keeps each search result for 12 hours, so the same search repeated in that time is answered from the saved copy. To prevent misuse, the server also counts how many searches each account (or network address) makes in a few minutes; this count is kept in memory only and is not saved. Your cases and history stay in your account until you delete them.

          05How your data is protected

          • Every page and every search requires you to be signed in, and the server checks your sign-in on each request.
          • Your data is stored in Google Firebase (Firestore). Security rules make sure only you can read or change your own cases, history, tracked people and notifications.
          • The keys for the search services are kept on the server and are never sent to your browser.
          • When it is switched on, Firebase App Check (which uses Google reCAPTCHA) helps block automated abuse.

          06Your choices and rights

          Your work belongs to you. You can delete single cases, delete single searches or clear your whole search history, and stop tracking anyone at any time. In Settings → Data & privacy you can export all of your data, delete all your investigations, or delete your account.

          07Children and changes

          The platform is for adults (18 or older) doing professional or lawful research. We do not knowingly collect information from children under 13. If this policy changes, the effective date at the top of this page is updated.

              Ethical OSINT principles.

              How to use public information fairly: check before you conclude, respect people’s privacy, and handle what you find with care.

              Contents
                Contents

                  01Our ethical framework

                  Open-source intelligence (OSINT) gives researchers powerful tools. With them comes a duty to act lawfully, fairly and only as far as the task really needs.

                  Allowed uses

                  • Lawful investigations and fraud or identity checks
                  • Security research and threat investigation
                  • Journalism: checking public claims and public figures
                  • Academic research on public online activity
                  • Due diligence on organisations and their public links

                  Not allowed

                  • Harassment, stalking or intimidation — including keyword alerts that follow a private person
                  • Doxxing — publishing someone’s address, phone number or email to expose or harm them
                  • Credit, job, housing or insurance screening (FCRA)
                  • Getting around privacy settings or into private accounts
                  • Stealing passwords or accessing accounts without permission

                  02Check sources before you conclude

                  A matching name or username does not prove that two accounts belong to the same person. Always open the original source, and look for more than one piece of evidence — such as the same display name, location, website or links between profiles — before you connect anything to your subject.

                  03Use the platform’s safeguards

                  • Match labels are not proof. “Exact match”, “Username variation”, “Possibly related” and “Other person” explain why a result was found; they do not confirm identity. Accounts labelled “Other person” belong to someone else unless you can show otherwise.
                  • Similar names stay apart. The platform never merges look-alike people or organisations into your subject — do not merge them yourself without evidence.
                  • “Not found” means not found. The platform does not guess missing details, and you should not either. Gender is only shown when a profile states it; a place is only recorded when a source states it.
                  • Organisation facts show their confidence. “Confirmed by several sources”, “Single source” and “Sources disagree” tell you how far to trust a fact.
                  • Record what you checked. Mark results as Raw, Relevant or Validated, and keep the Audit tab as the record of how you reached your conclusion.

                  04Handle personal information with care

                  The platform can show public contact details (emails and phone numbers) and places connected to a person. Even when they are public, treat them with care:

                  • Keep only what your task needs, and delete cases you no longer need.
                  • Keep your cases, reports and exports confidential. Share view-only links only with people who are allowed to see them, and stop sharing when they no longer need access.
                  • Use keyword alerts for topics, events and public figures related to your work — not to keep watch on private individuals.
                  • Never publish or pass on a person’s contact details or location to expose, harass or harm them.

                  Legal process & agency guidelines.

                  For government and law enforcement officers: what the platform is, what information it keeps, and what legal process we require.

                  Contents
                    Contents

                      01What the platform is

                      The OSINT Investigation Platform is software that searches public sources on behalf of its users: search engines through SerpApi (Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, YouTube, news, images, maps and trends), public platform services (such as GitHub, Reddit, Mastodon and Bluesky), Wikidata, and the public pages of organisations’ own websites. The platform does not run its own database of personal records, criminal records or telecom data.

                      What the platform does not have

                      The platform does not collect, store or give access to:

                      • Private messages or chat logs
                      • Passwords or login details of any platform
                      • Private (non-public) social media accounts
                      • Phone company or subscriber records
                      • Phone or device location data
                      • Hidden, paid or non-public databases
                      • Facial recognition data

                      02Information kept about users

                      The only non-public information held for the platform is about its own users, stored in Google Firebase:

                      • Account details: email address, display name, user ID, sign-in method and account creation date (Firebase Authentication).
                      • Saved cases: the investigations a user created — the public results they contain, the user’s evidence levels and each case’s audit log.
                      • Account activity: the user’s search history, tracked people and organisations, notifications and settings.

                      A user’s search history is stored in their own account, which they can delete. Search results are kept on the server for up to 12 hours only, to avoid running the same search twice. The server’s technical output, and the standard logs of our hosting and service providers, may also contain request details for a limited time.

                      03Legal process requirements

                      We require valid legal process before releasing any non-public user information:

                      1Subpoena(or local equivalent) — for basic account details, such as email address and account creation date.
                      2Court orderFor a user’s search history and activity records.
                      3Search warrantFor the contents of a user’s saved cases.

                      The public information shown in the platform can be found by anyone through the original sources; each result keeps a link to where it came from.

                      For official requests or service of legal process:Contact us through the Help Center
